STEP 1 · ABOUT 3 MIN

What is AI?

Definition

AI (artificial intelligence) is a computer program that can do things we usually expect a person to do, like write, answer questions, or recognize faces.

Most AI today works by learning from examples: it looks at lots of examples (samples, like photos of dogs), finds the patterns (things that show up again and again), and uses them to guess about something new.

A program that follows rules

A person writes exact instructions for every situation. Example: a calculator.

AI that learns from examples

It finds patterns in thousands of examples. Example: face unlock, which learned what your face looks like from pictures of it.

How ChatGPT (a popular chatbot made by the company OpenAI) writes: it doesn't look answers up. It guesses the most likely next word, over and over, like your phone keyboard's suggested words. That's why it can sound sure and still be wrong.

Key words

Program
A set of instructions that tells a computer what to do. Apps are programs.
Example
One sample the computer looks at, like one photo of a dog.
Pattern
Something that shows up again and again.
Rule
An exact instruction a person writes for the computer.
Chatbot
A program you type messages to that types back.
STEP 2 · ABOUT 2 MIN

A short history of AI

The big change: computers went from following rules to learning from examples.

  1. 1950
    The question

    "Can machines think?"

    Scientist Alan Turing asks this question in a famous paper (a written report on research).

  2. 1956
    A name

    The field gets its name

    Scientists at a meeting at Dartmouth College, in New Hampshire, call the new field (area of study) "artificial intelligence."

  3. 1966
    Rules

    The first chatbot: ELIZA

    ELIZA looks for certain words in your message and answers with a reply a person wrote ahead of time. Some people thought it understood them. It didn't. You'll try a bot like it in Step 3.

  4. 1997
    Rules + speed

    A computer wins at chess

    Deep Blue, a chess computer built by the company IBM, beats world champion Garry Kasparov by checking millions of possible moves very fast.

  5. 2022
    Learning

    ChatGPT is released

    ChatGPT is released to the public. It learned patterns from huge amounts of writing. No one wrote its replies as rules.

    STEP 4 · ABOUT 4 MIN

    From ELIZA to today: LLMs and agents

    Every chatbot since ELIZA does the same basic job: read your message, then write a reply. What changed is how it decides what to say, and what else it can do. Each step below builds on the one before it.

    Key words

    Trained
    Taught by being shown lots of examples.
    Text
    Written words.
    Tool
    Another program an AI can use, like a web search or a calendar.
    Goal
    The result you want, like "plan a trip."
    Safety rules
    Limits that stop a chatbot from giving harmful or dangerous answers.
    1. 1

      Rule chatbot (ELIZA, 1966)

      A person writes every rule. The bot spots a key word and gives a reply written ahead of time.
    2. 2

      Learning from examples

      Adds: the computer finds patterns in examples instead of following hand-written rules.
    3. 3

      LLM (large language model)

      Adds: learns patterns from a huge amount of writing, so it can write about almost anything.
    4. 4

      Chatbot app (like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini)

      Adds: an LLM trained further to hold a helpful conversation, remember what you said earlier in the chat, and follow safety rules.
    5. 5

      AI agent

      Adds: tools, and the power to take actions on its own, step by step, toward a goal you give it.
    Key word

    LLM: large language model

    An AI that learned patterns from a huge amount of writing (books, websites, and more) so it can predict the next word again and again.

    • Large: trained on an enormous amount of text.
    • Language: it works with words.
    • Model: the set of patterns it learned.

    The LLM is the part inside chatbot apps like ChatGPT that writes the words.

    Key word

    AI agent

    An AI that doesn't just answer. It takes actions to reach a goal, using tools like web search, apps, or online forms, one step at a time.

    • Chatbot: "Here are some tips for planning a trip."
    • Agent: searches for flights, compares prices, and fills in the form to buy a ticket, then waits for you to say yes.

    Because agents act, a person must check their work before anything final happens. That's the idea behind the H1–H5 scale from Module 0: a person is always responsible for the result.

    One thing that hasn't changed since 1966: people tend to believe a chatbot understands them because it sounds human. That's called the ELIZA effect: trusting a computer more because it talks like a person. Today's chatbots are far more capable than ELIZA, but sounding sure is still not the same as being right.
